The story

Every oath is written down somewhere

For thousands of years the god-king Ilmarion the Just has kept the peace across the Oathlands. Now something is changing at the heart of his kingdom, and four rival gods are certain they know who should come next.

Far from the thrones, in the soot-stained iron town of Hearthmoor, a blacksmith named Teiran Vaele has debts to pay. He is about to find out that the fate of the realms and the fate of one small forge are tied by the same old promise.

The seven realms

  • Caelmar: marble and law. Seat of Caelthar, who believes the world must be kept as it was.
  • The Far Waste: empty, untracked country of wind, dust and long silences.
  • Aurendell: a terraced island of pale stone where the god-king keeps court.
  • Elowmere: orchard and river country, home of Sylvaine, god of mercy and harvest.
  • The Hollow Roads: lamplit crossroads, Oriel's country and the Lampwrights'.
  • The Iron Country: hills of forge and coal-smoke. Hearthmoor, where the story begins, sits here.
  • The Drowned Coast: harbor isles of Morvaen and the Tidebound.

The ages

Five ages of the world

The Oathlands count their history in ages, and the ages are not equal. One of them is longer than all the others put together, many times over.

  1. The Age of Chaos

    About 400 years

    Before the gods ruled, nothing held still. Seasons, oaths, and the line between the living and the dead shifted without warning. Wars between the lesser gods and the first mortals ended whole peoples, and no peace lasted long enough to be named.

  2. The Settling

    About 20 years

    In a few desperate years the wars ended and the world was made to hold steady. What was promised to bring that about, and by whom, the old songs no longer say.

  3. The Long Peace

    About 6,000 years

    Ilmarion the Just ruled, and the world prospered. Four gods rose to their own domains, kingdoms grew up around each of them, and the old strangeness faded into stories. The peace was so long that most people stopped believing there had ever been anything else.

  4. The Dying Age

    About 35 years

    Ilmarion begins to die. His power leaks into the world and the laws he held steady start to slip: seasons misfire, wells turn bitter, and a sworn oath weighs no more than a breath. The four claimants begin their war, and small, warm things called Emberkin appear near the hearths. A smith named Teiran was born as it began, so he has never known a steady world.

    The story takes place in its final years

  5. What comes after

    Not yet told

    No one in the Oathlands can say what the next age will be, and neither can we. That is what the story is for.

The Long Peace lasted about fourteen times as long as the Age of Chaos and the Settling put together.
